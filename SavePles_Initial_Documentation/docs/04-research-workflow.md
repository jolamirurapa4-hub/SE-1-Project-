# Research workflow

Review state, clearance status and visibility are independent.

## Lifecycle

1. Create a private project and identify authors, organization and supervisor.
2. Draft the proposal, methodology, analysis plan and data management arrangements.
3. Submit an immutable proposal version to an assigned reviewer.
4. Receive approval, rejection or a request for changes. Resubmission creates a new review round.
5. Identify and obtain applicable ethics, site, community and other permissions. Record evidence, dates and scope.
6. Begin the relevant collection activities only when required clearances are verified. A not-applicable decision needs an authorized reason.
7. Import permitted data, document cleaning steps and run analyses.
8. Draft conclusions and limitations; submit an exact final report version.
9. Complete final report review and resolve requested changes.
10. Request archival release, select authorized visibility and confirm rights.
11. Complete release checks and publish the selected version when authorized.

For retrospective archive imports, record historical approval evidence and missing information accurately; importing an existing paper must never imply retrospective ethics clearance.

## State models

Proposal and final report submissions each use: draft, submitted, under review, changes requested, approved, rejected or withdrawn.

Clearance records use: required, pending, verified, expired, revoked or not applicable.

Project visibility uses: private, invited collaborators, organization-only or public release. Public release applies to selected approved artifacts, not the entire workspace.

## Submission integrity

A submission records its report revision, included files, dataset references, assigned reviewer and timestamp. Reviewers annotate that frozen version. Changes to a draft do not alter a pending or approved submission. Material changes require a fresh review according to institutional policy.

## Release integrity

Approval alone does not publish content. A release records author authorization, approved version, rights declaration, selected license or access terms, moderation result and a stable identifier. The researcher can keep an approved project private.

New public versions retain their own identifiers and review records. Withdrawals remove normal public access and record a reason; retention of a metadata notice depends on the institution's policy and any safety concern.

## Notifications

Provide in-app events for invitations, submissions, decisions and release changes. Email notifications may be added after provider and delivery arrangements are agreed. Sensitive research details must not be included in notification previews.
